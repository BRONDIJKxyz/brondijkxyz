The Action Loop

It starts as a warning loop, and that is where most of these systems stop. The difference here is the button underneath the warning: the alert comes with the action already prepared.

What this is about

Right now everything is reactive. I ask: check my email, where is this order. I look in Exact myself to see whether that PO has been sent. I open dashboards to see whether things are going well.

What I want is the other way around. A loop that keeps running as a warning, and then a button with an action on it. Everything is connected: the purchasing email, Exact, sales. The agent runs past all of it constantly, comes forward when something is going on, and hands the team the thing to press.

Nobody should need to open a dashboard. The alert comes to the team, with the consequence and the action already attached.

Warning first, then a button

A warning on its own still leaves all the work with me: open Exact, find the PO, write the mail, remember to check back. The button is where the difference sits. The same agent that spotted it writes the change back into the system it came from, or puts the message in front of me ready to send.

From reactive to ahead of it

How it works today
  • I have to remember to go looking
  • Search the mailbox: anything yet from Flexport or the forwarder
  • Search Exact: has this PO been sent
  • The dashboard only tells me once it is already late
  • Follow-ups sit there until someone misses them
How it should work
  • The loop keeps looking, including at what does not happen
  • A push to the team while there is still something to do
  • The consequence is already calculated
  • The action sits inside the alert, I only have to click
  • Not followed up is an alert in itself

How it works, in five layers

  1. 01

    Everything is connected

    The purchasing email, Exact (including MRP and production orders), sales, the subscriptions in Recharge, the forwarders and the warehouses. Without those sources side by side the agent cannot connect anything.

  2. 02

    The loop runs constantly

    Not a question I ask, but a check that keeps running. The important part: the agent watches just as closely for what is missing and what quietly changed. No mail from the forwarder, no ready confirmation from the supplier, no shipment on the way, or a new price landing in the mailbox. That is the signal.

  3. 03

    The warning comes forward

    A push message. Email, Slack or something else, but it has to come to the team. With the PO attached: look at this order, it probably has a problem, or: you need to make a call today.

  4. 04

    The consequence is already calculated

    If you do nothing this production order cannot run, and then you are short in these weeks. Ideally the AI says all of it: based on the next 90 days of Recharge data you cannot run this production, we still have this much on hand, that keeps the subscriptions supplied until this date.

  5. 05

    The button does the work

    This is the part that matters. From the alert I click, and the agent executes: the new price with its start date into Exact so every new purchase order picks it up, the message to the supplier asking why it is not ready, the mail to the customers whose order will be late. Anything leaving the company is a draft I read and send myself. Anything internal it can write, with my click as the approval.

Two kinds of signal

Something is missing

No shipment on the way, no ready confirmation, no tracking, no answer to a follow-up. Nothing arrives, so nothing triggers me today. This is the type that costs the most, because silence looks like everything is fine.

Something changed

A price adjustment, a new lead time, a moved ETA, a changed minimum order quantity. It lands in the mailbox as a normal email and needs a decision, with an effective date attached that nobody is tracking.

How a check is written down

The chocolate powder case is the hard one to write down, because nothing happened. There is no delay mail, no bad ETA, no exception to catch. What there is: no idea of shipping anywhere, and the PO was never marked as on the way. The check has to be built on the absence of evidence measured against a clock.

So every check gets written down in the same seven lines. Absence on the left, a date to compare it to on the right.

Rule
PO-01, no evidence of departure
Watches
every open PO in Exact that MRP still needs
Evidence
a ready confirmation from the supplier, or a booking or ETA from the forwarder, or the PO marked shipped or on the way in Exact, or a tracking number
Fires when
none of those four exist, and today has passed (date needed, minus inbound, minus transit, minus production, minus buffer)
Verdict
nothing is moving, so this will probably arrive too late, X days short
Buttons
message the supplier, cost out air freight, snooze until a date
Re-check
daily, and the severity rises as the gap grows

The clock is the part that keeps it quiet. The same missing evidence means nothing at 20 weeks out and means trouble at 8, so the rule works backwards from the date MRP needs the goods and only fires once the remaining time is shorter than the route still takes.

The field nobody fills

One thing to build in from the start: "not found" is not the same as "not there". If the PO status in Exact is a field nobody maintains, a rule leaning on it alone flags every order we have. So a rule needs at least two independent sources silent before it fires, and it has to be able to say I could not check this rather than reporting all clear. Per source we write down whether it is actually maintained, and how stale is too stale.

Case one: the chocolate powder

This is how it runs from signal to action. Figures below are illustrative.

Signal
No mail from the forwarder about this PO, and the supplier has not even said the goods are ready. Or a mail does come in: delayed.
Requirement
Exact MRP: this product is needed as soon as stock runs out, in 14 weeks.
Check
Is anything on the way? No booking, no tracking, and the PO was never marked as on the way in Exact either. Rule PO-01.
Lead time
This supplier is in China. Sea freight plus inbound no longer fits inside 14 weeks.
Conclusion
This PO probably has a problem. Something needs to happen now, not in a month.
Impact
Without this ingredient the week 47 production order cannot run. Short in weeks 49 and 50. With current stock the subscriptions can still be supplied until early December.
Push
Warning to Slack and mail, with the options: call now, or air freight that still makes week 46.
Action
Message to the supplier prepared as a draft. Mail to the customers with a late order prepared as a draft.
Watch
No response within two days? The same PO comes back: this has not been followed up, should you not sort this out.

Case two: a price adjustment

Same loop, different trigger, and the clearest case for the button. A mail comes in about a price change for cacao powder or coconut powder. The agent should find what the period is and what the adjusted price should be, and bring that forward as a decision instead of letting it sit in the mailbox.

Signal
A mail from the supplier announcing a price adjustment, with a period attached.
Extract
The agent pulls out what matters: which item, the old price, the new price, the percentage, from which date it applies and for how long it is quoted. So in this case four months from now.
Context
What it does to the cost price per kg and per finished unit, and which open PO's still run on the old price.
Push
Cacao powder has a price adjustment. Do you want to send a mail to the supplier saying you do not agree, or do you want me to push it through?
If pushed through
The new price and its start date go into Exact, and from that date every new purchase order is created on the new price automatically.
If not
A draft to the supplier saying we do not agree, and the item stays open until there is an answer.
Watch
If nothing is decided before the start date, it comes back. A price change that quietly takes effect is the same failure as a shipment that quietly never left.
Slack · supply-and-operations 08:40

What changed

Supplier announced 4.60 to 4.95 per kg (+7.6%), effective 1 January 2027, quoted for 12 months. Mail received this morning.

What it means

Your call

Push it through, and every new PO from 1 January is created on the new price in Exact. Or reply that we do not agree and keep it open.

What the system keeps track of

  • Per PO: is anything on the way, is there a ready confirmation, is there tracking, does the ETA still match what MRP needs.
  • Per production order: are all ingredients in on time, and if not, which one pushes it out.
  • Per SKU: when does stock run out, and how long can the subscriptions keep running on the queued charges.
  • Per commercial term: current price, agreed period, announced change, effective date, and which PO's it applies to.
  • Per alert: which button was pressed, what it wrote, and whether anything came back. An open alert nobody responds to has to keep coming back.
